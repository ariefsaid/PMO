# Judge the rendered whole

Read whole at step 8; a green meter is the floor, and the before-shot is a loss baseline rather than the quality bar.

## Director's written critique

Open the current render at actual size and at a 25% thumbnail, then answer every question with the visible part and evidence.

1. What job does this person arrive to do, what do I see first, and does the biggest block answer it?
2. What is today's finding, and can the person take its next step beside the evidence?
3. Does the first desktop viewport show every brief-named block's header and first rows or figure, with enough substance to work?
4. Do title, figures, sections, body and secondary text form distinct ranks, or does everything have the same weight?
5. Does the grid close at both canvas edges, and do row endings, card interiors, headings and corners contain intentional space rather than blank bands?
6. Is each visual warranted by its data and decision, and can every series, denominator, unit, period and comparison be identified without hovering?
7. Do repeated figures agree across the screen and its destination, including known-zero, missing and partial cases?
8. Is every Preserve item still visible or discoverably reachable at its recorded home, and are all drops backed by the owner's words?
9. Does each place confirm its name across menu, breadcrumb and title, and does each concept have one word in both languages?
10. Are caveats stated once in their proper scope with a remedy, while consequences, optionality and information needed before a decision remain present?
11. On phone, does the task lead, do labels travel with values, and do evidence and controls stay readable, reachable and clear of overlays?
12. What would an exacting designer screenshot and send back: a near-tangent, a cramped label, a drifting figure, a clipped control or a whole screen without a first glance?

- Write a pass/fail and the deciding capture or journey for each answer, because adjectives alone cannot direct a repair.
- Judge every required role/state, both themes and languages, because one good desktop state cannot stand in for the matrix.
- Fix the most consequential visible failure first within the one review/one fix budget, because many little improvements cannot rescue a broken composition.
- If the fix list exceeds about five or the whole still has no hierarchy, return to composition and the Director's scope decision, because another card patch repeats the failure.
- When an eye-found issue names a repeatable class, dispatch a cheap app-wide read-only audit that day, because the screenshot is one instance rather than the denominator.

## Impeccable critique pass

For a recomposed screen, the reviewer runs one `impeccable critique` pass (its `reference/critique.md`) and writes its design judgement first, detector evidence second, kept apart; the detector never stands in for the judgement.

## Blind fresh eyes at a batch boundary

- Before dispatch, the Director freezes a private hidden list of owner concerns and acceptance targets, because a reviewer shown the list can only demonstrate checklist compliance.
- Give a fresh design-grade reviewer only the running URL, safe test accounts, business roles/jobs and scope; keep the hidden list, previous reports and authors' diagnoses out of its context.
- Require first-glance observation, actual-size renders, measurements and real task walks across the batch, because a flow review and a visual review find different things.
- After its report is final, the Director scores each hidden concern as found unprompted yes/no and gone/reduced/unchanged/new, with a quotation and capture, because “fresh eyes passed” is otherwise untestable.
- Mark unreproducible concerns separately and supply the required fixture before claiming them gone, because sparse seed data is not proof of repair.
- Resolve every unchanged concern and every new issue of comparable weight before acceptance; record any owner-authorized deferral explicitly, because a score alone does not restore a loss.
- Keep the list and score private, because exposing the answer key spoils future blind runs and may publish sensitive evidence.

## What the owner receives

- Put actual before/after images first at matched width, role, theme, language, data, period and crop, because unequal baselines can hide losses or invent improvements.
- Include actual-size first-screen and complete-pane captures at desktop and phone, plus the local URL, because a single combined image is not enough to click through.
- Report each visible move, its evidence grade, floor disposition and any unverified state briefly, because the report must claim only what the picture or journey proves.
- Keep reference identities and comparisons in the private evidence bundle, because tracked issue comments are public.
- State no measured business outcome unless the run measured it; an image can show placement and hierarchy, not conversion or trust.
- Record the owner's explicit pick/acceptance and any correction, because a successor needs the decision that ended the run.

**Complete:** the Director's written critique and blind batch score are recorded, losses are reconciled, and the owner accepts the before/after and real click-through.
