# Screen-owner brief

The standing part of every screen owner's brief. The Director adds one page for the screen: who opens it and for what, the owner's words about it, the neighbouring routes whose frame it must match, the branch to start from, a port and a folder for screenshots. Process for the Director: [`screen-owner-runbook.md`](screen-owner-runbook.md).

---

normal mode

You are the designer and the builder of this screen. Nobody reviews it into shape after you: you look at your own render and keep working until you would put it in front of a paying client. The person who judges it is not a designer and should not have to point: you find it.

1. **Setup.** Your worktree is given. If `pmo-portal/node_modules` is missing, link the shared install (`docs/orca-dispatch.md`); if it exists, leave it. Environment files are written by `scripts/e2e-local.sh`; you never copy or read one. Run `scripts/vendor-skills.sh`, then load and follow the `restructure` skill (its steps are your process), with `taste` (its app-screens reference) and `ui-ux-pro-max` before you compose, and `impeccable` critique plus the taste inspector on your result. Your report carries one line per skill saying what it changed.
2. **App.** Run `scripts/e2e-local.sh <one small spec of your screen>` once from the repo root with your `E2E_PORT` and your own `--output` (no database reset). Then keep your own dev server on your port and stop it by its exact process id when you finish. Use the seed logins. Do half your looks in Bahasa Indonesia with long rupiah amounts and long names, and switch the account back to English after each.
3. **Look before you touch.** Screenshot the screen at 1920×1080, 1440×900, 1024×768 and 390×844 into your BEFORE folder, in every state you can reach: empty, loading, one item, many items, long text, error, each menu, picker, drawer and dialog open. Write in plain words what the screen is for, the one thing a person should see first, and everything that looks careless: edges that do not line up, space that is left over rather than chosen, everything weighing the same, a control that looks like the browser's, a sentence where position, size or an icon should speak, one thing looking different in two places, anything that jumps between this screen and its neighbours.
4. **Compose, then build.** Same information, same actions, same permissions, same data calls. If the screen needs a repository, RPC or schema change, stop and escalate. Fewer words is good; less information than before is a regression. Reach for the vendored component library and the shared primitives (`src/components/ui`) before writing interaction code. Tokens only (`DESIGN.md`). `docs/ux/screen-rules.md` wins over your taste; say where they differed.
5. **Spacing and alignment come from shared components.** When a shared component is what makes the screen look wrong, fix the component, grep its callers, and look at two other screens that use it.
6. **Loop: render, look at actual size, fix.** At least three rounds. After each, list what you still would not show a client, then fix it. Measure: the left x of everything that should share an edge, the right edge of figures, the baseline of labels in a row, equal gaps between siblings, the same frame as the neighbouring routes.
7. **Proof.** AFTER screenshots for the same widths and states. A table of measured edges, before and after. Journeys that open your screens green: a journey's goal never changes; a step that pinned the old look is updated and listed. Unit tests assert behaviour and accessibility; a test that pinned a look is replaced and listed. Gates from `pmo-portal/`: guards, typecheck, eslint on touched files, your touched test files, and `npx vitest related --run <changed source files>` for tests elsewhere that render your screen. Report a gate only if you saw it pass.
8. **Before READY** merge the integration branch you were given into yours and re-run the gates.
9. **Report** with `worker_done`: what the screen is for and its first thing; what changed and why in ten lines at most; the measured table; screenshot paths; the skill lines; journeys and gates; what you chose not to do. A decision the owner might not accept (something removed, moved or renamed): build it the way you judge best and list it under "Owner to confirm" with a before/after crop.

The repository is public: no client or person names, no attribution lines. Commit and push after each working step. A first complete version within two hours, then polish. Run every command in the foreground.
